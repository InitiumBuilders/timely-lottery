The Dance

Hover or tap each practice to reveal its deeper wisdom

01 // Get the beat.

Before you disturb the system in any way, watch how it behaves. If it's a piece of music or a dance, a social system or an ecosystem, learn its beat, its natural rhythms and repetitions, its structure, its history. Starting with the beat — watching and listening — is the place to begin.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
02 // Listen to the wisdom of the system.

Aid and encourage the forces and structures that help the system run itself. Notice how many of those structures are themselves embedded in larger systems with their own dynamics. Pay attention to what the system tells you, and try to maximize the capacity of the system to signal its own needs.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
03 // Expose your mental models to the open air.

Remember, always, that everything you know, and everything everyone knows, is only a model. Get your model out there where it can be shot at. Invite others to challenge your assumptions and add their own. The key to navigate any complex system is to keep questioning what you believe you know.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
04 // Stay humble. Stay a learner.

Systems surprises us because we think we know more than we do. We know what we see; we don't see what we don't see. A truly humble scientist or a truly great athlete knows that perfection is approached, never reached — that there is always more to learn, always a way to be better next time.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
05 // Honor and protect information.

A system can't function well if its information streams are corrupted. Pretending information is better or worse than it is misleads everyone into making poor decisions. The best gift you can give a system is accurate information — at the right time, in the right place, in the right form.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
06 // Locate responsibility in the system.

Look for the system's own feedback mechanisms that could restore the system's behavior if it goes off track. Don't look for who's to blame; find the feedback loop that drives the problematic behavior and see if you can redirect it. Responsibility means response-ability — the capacity to respond.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
07 // Make feedback policies for feedback systems.

Design learning into the management process. Create policies that change depending on the state of the system. Design monitoring into the policy, along with triggers for change. Take into account not just the current state of the system but the trend of the system's behavior over time.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
08 // Pay attention to what is important, not just what is quantifiable.

Our culture, obsessed with numbers, has given us the idea that what we can measure is more real than what we cannot measure. But think about what has been important in your own life — family, love, beauty, honor, joy. Most of what really matters is beyond quantification.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
09 // Go for the good of the whole.

Remember that hierarchies exist to serve the lower levels. Don't optimize parts of systems or subsystems while ignoring the whole. Don't be distracted by the pull of short-term thinking. Aim always toward a better whole system — one that produces more of what it's supposed to produce.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
10 // Expand time horizons.

In system after system, the delayed effects are ignored or given too little weight. The time horizon of governments rarely extends beyond the next election, of corporations beyond the next quarterly report. Yet the world is full of long-term consequences that show up decades or generations later.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
11 // Expand thought horizons.

Expand the boundary of caring. Include in your mental model not just what you can see and quantify but also the things you care about, even if they are hard to measure. Invite into the conversation people who represent different viewpoints and can contribute different ideas.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
12 // Expand the boundary of caring.

Living successfully in a world of systems requires more of us than our ability to calculate. It requires our full humanity — the rationality of our minds, the intuition of our spirits, the compassion of our hearts. Cultivate a caring that reaches through space, time, and species.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
13 // Celebrate complexity.

Systems are marvelous at producing complex behavior from simple rules. They produce structure without design, order without planning. Watch how the system runs itself. Marvel at it. Be grateful for complexity. Modern science has shown that the world is far more interesting and surprising than we imagine.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
14 // Hold fast to the goal of goodness.

It is not enough to understand the world. The point is to make it better. If we truly and deeply believe in the goal of goodness — if we are truly intent on making the world better — then we will not be tempted into sophisticated justifications for cynicism and selfishness. Dance with grace and integrity.

— Donella Meadows, Dancing With Systems
